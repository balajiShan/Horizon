# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of cybersecurity news for a reader who wants to know what is actively exploited, what is newly discovered, and what requires action.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Critical.** Actively exploited zero-days, widespread breaches, supply-chain compromises, or flaws in widely deployed software with urgent action required.
- **7-8: High value.** Significant vulnerabilities with technical detail, novel attack techniques, detailed threat actor or malware analysis, major incident disclosures, or important patches.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Moderate vulnerabilities, useful research, policy changes, or incidents with limited reach.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine patch roundups, recycled advice, shallow coverage, or content dominated by vendor promotion.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, off-topic material, fear-driven headlines without substance, or purely promotional content.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider exploitability, scope of affected systems, evidence of active exploitation, availability of a fix, technical depth, and source credibility. Distinguish confirmed incidents and vendor-verified flaws from unverified claims or attacker boasts. Prefer primary sources and technical detail (CVE IDs, affected versions, indicators) over commentary. Do not reward exaggerated headlines.

Use three to five specific topic tags (for example: ransomware, zero-day, supply-chain, cloud-security).
